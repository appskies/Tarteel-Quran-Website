2. Information We Collect

2.1 Information You Provide

The App does not require you to create an account. You can use it with an automatically generated anonymous identifier, and we do not collect a username or password. You may optionally provide:

  • Email address (optional): If you choose to enter it during onboarding or when contacting us, we use it to send you relevant updates and reply to support requests. On both iOS and Android, the App saves an onboarding email in our Firebase database (Cloud Firestore) in a document keyed by your Firebase UID (userEmails/{uid}), together with created and updated timestamps. On iOS a copy is also kept on your device as a support-reply cache. On Android a copy is also kept on your device and is included in the support emails you start from the App. The App does not forward newly submitted emails to subscription or attribution providers. You can skip this; the App works fully without it.
  • Support communications: The content of messages you send us for support or feedback, together with basic diagnostic details you agree to attach (app version, device model, OS version, language, and subscription status).
  • AI assistant input: The messages you type into the in-app AI assistant, which are sent to our AI service provider to generate a response (see §2.4 and §9). The AI assistant is available only to premium subscribers.
  • Reported AI replies (Android version code 9 and later): Only if you choose to report an AI assistant reply, the text of that reply, the reason you pick, your app language, the app version and the platform. We do not store your Firebase user ID with the report (see §9).
  • Referral information (Android): On Android, the App has a referral feature. Your share code is the first six characters of your Firebase UID. When you open the feature, the App creates a record for you in our database (ReferralManagerUsers/{uid}) that holds a credit balance. If you enter a friend's code, our server (a Firebase Cloud Function) records which Firebase UID referred you, the credit amount and the time, in a server-only record keyed by your Firebase UID, and adds credit to the referrer's balance. Whether a redemption currently earns credit depends on a server setting that we control. Referral information (iOS): the current iOS App does not offer a referral feature; earlier iOS versions may have associated referral credits and the history of promo codes used with your anonymous app identifier.
  • Preferences & reading progress: Your settings, bookmarks, reading goals, and Khatmah/tracking progress, stored on your device.

Anonymous sign-in and Firebase services that run from launch: When the App starts, it signs in to Firebase Authentication anonymously, which assigns a pseudonymous identifier (a Firebase UID) rather than creating a named account, and it reads its remote configuration from Firebase. Firebase also creates a Firebase Installations identifier for the App on your device. On Android, Firebase App Check (Google Play Integrity) attests requests from the App, and Firebase Cloud Messaging registers the device with Google to obtain a push token, which the App stores on your device only. These services are needed to run the App, they run from launch, and they are not switched off by any analytics or consent choice.

2.2 Information Collected Automatically

  • Device information: Device model, operating system and version, language and region settings, and device identifiers. On iOS these include the Identifier for Vendors (IDFV) and, with your permission (see §4), the Identifier for Advertisers (IDFA). On Android they include the Android advertising ID (where available and not deleted by you), the Google Play install referrer, and app-instance identifiers assigned by Firebase and AppsFlyer (see §4).
  • Usage & analytics data: How you interact with the App (features used, screens viewed, and session information) to understand and improve the App. Firebase Analytics also receives user properties such as premium status, app language, install cohort, notification opt-in and lifecycle stage.
  • Diagnostic & crash data: Crash reports and technical logs used to diagnose and fix problems.
  • Recitation audio: When you play or download a Quran recitation, the App requests the audio files from everyayah.com, a third-party host, on both iOS and Android. That host receives your IP address and the surah, ayah and reciter you request. We do not control its logging.
  • Location: If you grant location permission, the App uses your device location to calculate local prayer times and the Qibla direction. To find your city, the App sends your raw coordinates to the operating system's geocoder: on Android, the Android Geocoder and the fused location provider from Google Play services (which Google operates); on iOS, Apple's CLGeocoder. Those services are run by Google or Apple under their own policies. The App then sends only the city and country (not your coordinates) to its prayer-time content provider, AlAdhan. The App stores your last-known city, country and coordinates on your device so it can reuse them. On iOS, the nearby-mosque feature searches with Apple MapKit, which receives your search text and the map region. On Android, the nearby-mosque feature sends the search center's coordinates (approximate or precise, depending on the location available) to the third-party Overpass service at overpass-api.de, sends a city name to Nominatim at nominatim.openstreetmap.org if the device geocoder cannot resolve a city, and loads OpenStreetMap map tiles, which discloses the displayed map area and your IP address to the tile service (see §9). Advertising and attribution providers may also infer an approximate location from network or device signals, subject to your choices and their policies.
  • Purchase information: Records of in-app purchases and subscriptions, where offered. Payment is processed by the relevant app store (Apple App Store or Google Play); we do not receive your payment card details. RevenueCat, our subscription provider, starts each time the App launches (see §9).

2.3 Information We Do NOT Collect

  • We do not access your contacts, calendar, photos, or other personal files.
  • We do not collect payment card information. All payments are processed by the relevant app store (Apple App Store or Google Play).

2.4 Information About Religious Beliefs

Questions you type and your worship and reading records may reveal your religious beliefs, which many laws treat as special-category (sensitive) data. Your worship and reading records are kept on your device. The AI assistant is optional and is used only when you choose to send a message: AI questions are sent only when you ask a question in the assistant, including when you select a suggested question, and sending is your explicit action (see §9). Before your first AI message the App shows a notice that your message goes to OpenAI (on iOS through AIProxy, on Android through our server) and asks you to accept it; nothing is sent until you accept, declining keeps your draft and sends nothing, and your acceptance is stored on your device. Please do not include sensitive personal information in your questions that you do not want shared.

6. Data Retention

  • On-device data (preferences, bookmarks, reading progress) remains on your device and is removed when you delete the App.
  • Optional email, referral records, and Firebase guest account (iOS): On iOS, these are retained until you use Settings › Delete My Data (where available in your installed version) or ask us to delete them. The authenticated in-app flow removes the app-owned Firestore email record, referral credits and used promo-code history; queues a best-effort removal request for any email attribute an earlier App version sent to RevenueCat; deletes the Firebase Authentication guest account; and clears the locally cached email. RevenueCat's public SDK does not provide provider-side confirmation for that attribute request, so contact us at the address in §13 if you require confirmed provider deletion. A new Firebase guest session is then created so the App can continue operating.
  • Optional email, referral records, and Firebase guest account (Android): On Android, these are retained until you use Settings › Delete My Data or ask us to delete them. The in-app flow deletes your userEmails/{uid} document and your ReferralManagerUsers/{uid} record in one batch that the server must acknowledge, then deletes the Firebase Authentication guest account, and clears the email saved on your device. A new Firebase guest session is then created so the App can continue operating. If Firebase cannot confirm that the old guest account was deleted, the App tells you and shows a deletion reference to send us.
  • What Android Delete My Data does not delete: It does not delete or contact RevenueCat, AppsFlyer, LevelPlay or Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics, so analytics, crash and attribution data held by those providers stays with them under their own retention periods. It does not delete your referral redemption record (the server-only record of who referred you), which we remove only if you email us your deletion reference. It does not delete AI usage counters, which expire automatically (see below). In Android version code 9 and later it also does not delete AI replies you reported, because they are stored without your Firebase user ID and we cannot tell which ones are yours (see below and §9). It does not erase preferences and reading progress stored on your device, and it does not remove operational logs (see §9).
  • Consent choices (iOS version 1.0.15 and later, Android version code 8 and later): Your consent choices (the IAB TCF consent strings that Google's User Messaging Platform writes, and on iOS the result of the region check) and the "Do not sell or share" setting are stored on your device only. Delete My Data does not remove them. Deleting (uninstalling) the App removes them; you can change them any time in Settings.
  • AI usage counters: On Android, each daily AI usage counter in Firestore is set to expire approximately 72 hours after its last update and is automatically removed through an enabled time-to-live (TTL) policy. These counters contain usage metadata only, not your question or reply text.
  • Reported AI replies (Android version code 9 and later): A reply you report in the app is stored without your Firebase user ID and is automatically deleted about 90 days after the report (automatic deletion can lag). The related per-day report counter expires about one day after the end of its UTC day. See §9.
  • Subscription continuity: Deleting the Firebase guest account does not cancel a subscription through the Apple App Store or Google Play. Where RevenueCat manages a subscription, it retains a pseudonymous subscriber identifier and purchase/subscription history so paid access and purchase restoration continue to work. Newly submitted emails are not sent to RevenueCat.
  • Interrupted deletion recovery: On iOS, the App temporarily stores the deletion phase and old anonymous Firebase identifier on your device so an interrupted account deletion can finish on the next launch. After Firebase confirms account deletion, this recovery state is removed when a fresh guest session is created; it is not uploaded as a deletion receipt. If Firebase invalidates the local session without confirming server-side account deletion, the App conservatively keeps the old anonymous identifier on the device as a support reference, clearly reports that deletion is unconfirmed, and restores a separate usable guest session. Android keeps a similar on-device deletion record for recovery and for the deletion reference.
  • iOS AI client identifier: The random identifier the iOS App sends with AI requests (see §9) is stored in the iOS Keychain. It is not removed by Delete My Data and can survive deleting and reinstalling the App.
  • Operational logs: Cloud Functions logs (see §9) are kept by Google Cloud Logging under the project's log retention setting, which is 30 days by default unless a different period is configured.
  • Analytics/diagnostic data is retained for the period set by each provider and by our settings in the provider's console, and, where applicable, in aggregated or de-identified form.
  • Transaction and subscription records are retained by the relevant app store (Apple or Google Play), RevenueCat where used, and us as needed to provide purchase access, restore purchases, prevent fraud, and meet financial, tax, or other legal obligations.

AI Assistant Data

On Android, your current message is sent to our Firebase HTTPS callable aiChat in the us-central1 region, which calls OpenAI on the server with your message and a server-side instruction. On iOS, your current message is sent through AIProxy to OpenAI. We do not attach your email, location, purchase data, past chat history, or Firebase UID to the request sent to OpenAI; anything you include in the current message itself is sent as part of that message. The AI assistant is shown only to premium subscribers.

On Android, questions are sent only when you ask a question in the assistant. This includes selecting a suggested question. On Android, our server records a daily usage counter linked to your pseudonymous Firebase UID and operational metadata (request outcome, model, input character count, token counts, and latency), but does not record the message or reply text, except for an AI reply you choose to report in Android version code 9 and later (next paragraph).

Reporting an AI response (Android version code 9 and later). If you tap Report on an AI assistant reply and confirm, the app sends that reply's text, the reason you chose (offensive or inappropriate, inaccurate or misleading, or something else), your app language, the app version and the platform ("android") to a Firebase Cloud Function so we can review the response and improve the assistant. The report does not include your question, your email address, your Firebase user ID or any device identifier, although the reply text may itself repeat words from your question. It is stored in Firestore under a random document ID, without your Firebase user ID, and it is deleted automatically about 90 days after it is created (Firestore's automatic deletion can lag the exact expiry time). To limit abuse, a separate counter keyed to your pseudonymous Firebase user ID records how many reports that identity submitted per UTC day, is capped at 20 per day and expires one day after the end of that UTC day (Firestore's automatic deletion can lag). The counter is not used to look up reports, but because it and the report are written together, someone with administrative access to our database could, while the counter still exists, match your most recent report to your pseudonymous ID by its write time. After the counter has been deleted the report cannot be traced to you, so we generally cannot find or delete a particular person's report on request. If the report cannot reach our servers, the app instead prepares an email to appskiesltd@gmail.com that you review and send yourself.

OpenAI states that data submitted through its API is not used to train its models unless you opt in, and that limited abuse-monitoring logs may be retained, normally for up to 30 days, with exceptions described in its API data-usage policy. These logs may include message and reply content. On Android, our OpenAI requests set store: false to limit application-state retention; this does not disable OpenAI's abuse-monitoring logs.

Server Logs

Our Cloud Functions write operational logs to Google Cloud Logging. These logs include your Firebase UID and request metadata (outcome, model, input character count, token counts and latency for AI requests), and standard platform request information. Message and reply text is not written to these logs by our code. When you report an AI response (Android version code 9 and later), our code logs only the outcome and the reason code, not the reply text and not your Firebase UID. Retention is described in §6.
